Sentiment-Based Detection of Customer Dissatisfaction in Code-Switched Taglish E-Commerce Reviews Using XLM-RoBERTa

Fine-tuning cross-lingual transformer models to identify genuine customer dissatisfaction and multi-label complaint types in code-switched Filipino-English Shopee reviews.

Research Problem & Context

Tagalog-English code-switching (Taglish) dominates informal Philippine digital commerce on platforms like Shopee, yet off-the-shelf sentiment models struggle with its token-level language alternation, incentive-driven noise, and sarcasm that inverts surface polarity. Standard polarity classification fails to isolate actionable feedback for merchants.

Methodology & Pipeline Design

Serving as Project Lead alongside Christian Michael Delos Santos, Ko Han Lee, and Julian Edison Yalung under adviser Bernard C. Fabro, our team curated and annotated a dataset of 10,510 Taglish e-commerce reviews from Shopee.

We engineered a rigorous two-stage classification framework: Stage 1 fine-tunes XLM-RoBERTa to categorize reviews into positive, negative, neutral, and mixed sentiments. Stage 2 expands negative reviews into a multi-label classification structure, capturing co-occurring operational complaints such as damaged items, late delivery, wrong items, poor product quality, and poor seller service.

Evaluation & Metrics

The models are rigorously benchmarked against baseline architectures (Polyglot, VADER, mBERT, GPT-3, GPT-3.5) across classification metrics including accuracy, precision, recall, macro-F1, micro-F1, macro/micro-precision, macro/micro-recall, and hamming loss, utilizing stratified k-fold cross-validation and paired samples t-tests.
